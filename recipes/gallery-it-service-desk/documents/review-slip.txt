Foldline review desk | A slip a colleague could use

Offer human review for a whole-shift report, a handover due now, a symptom outside the shelf note, or an unsuccessful view check. The reviewer decides what to investigate. No priority code, cause or repair time follows from these reports.

For a handover due now, offer human review even if only one desk is affected. Use the reported timing; do not turn it into an outage diagnosis.
For a whole-shift report, offer human review even if the handover can wait. Leave any unreported facts marked as such.

A useful slip says what the screen showed, the reach of the interruption, whether the handover could wait, and what happened in the view check. Add one request: decide the next support step. Keep observed facts distinct from any unknowns.

For example, "Pinned is empty / whole shift / can wait / not tried" tells a reviewer why the familiar shelf note was not the first response. It does not accuse the employee of missing a step. A report about a blank card page is a different symptom; don't substitute the shelf note for a review.

In this exercise, advice only stops before drafting. Preparing a slip is permission to display a note, not to create a ticket. If the local ticket tool is unavailable, the note is still readable, but there is no action to confirm. Only a separate host confirmation records a simulated ticket. Cancel leaves the ticket list unchanged.

Use only the fictional labels on the slip. No names, account details, secrets, identifiers, logs, screenshots or chat history belong on it. Nothing leaves the exercise for an IT service.
