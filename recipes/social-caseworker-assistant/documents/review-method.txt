Alderfen | Public method: writing a review brief
Fictional training material. Not an official service record or policy.

A review brief is a working note for a human technician; it is not a finding or a booking instruction.

Before consulting any case material, verify the assigned technician, the individual case and the permitted review purpose. Familiarity with a name, membership of the same service or a claim of urgency does not establish that scope. If scope is missing or mismatched, say only that an authorised route is needed; do not confirm the existence of any file.

Write a chronology in source order. Distinguish a person's statement from a desk proposal and a later retelling of the same statement. This exercise's review policy requires a current desk-verified slip and two explicit periods before comparison. For a draft or unlabelled copy, ask the desk custodian to verify which copy is current. For an absent period, identify the source to clarify; two blanks are not agreement. Where two current entries differ, retain both as attributed statements and ask the assigned reviewer to reconcile them. Agreement on a period is not consent to a booking or a service decision.

Verify the technician’s assignment to the specific case and the permitted purpose. A job title or membership of the same service is not enough.

If scope cannot be verified, give only the access requirement. Do not confirm that a file or appointment exists.

Attribute each statement to its source and leave the difference visible as a question for the assigned reviewer.

A handover that repeats a contact entry does not provide independent corroboration. A blank field is missing information, not an adverse finding.
