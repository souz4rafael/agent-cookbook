Alderfen | Permission and questions about proposed times
Fictional training material. Not an official service record or policy.

A navigator can teach the general process to anyone. A plan-specific exercise is different: the person in the scenario or their listed supporter must have permission for that explanation. If the role or permission is missing, decline without saying whether any plan, appointment or referral exists. A role choice in this exercise proves no identity.

When a proposed time does not work, first distinguish the offer from a confirmed appointment. A useful question mentions the practical constraint without disclosing clinical details. For a travel barrier, ask whether a time compatible with available transport can be offered. For a communication barrier, ask whether the desk can provide the options in an accessible written format.

If no time has been offered, ask how to tell the desk about the constraint before an offer is made. A question for the person to use is not a message sent to a service. Do not request identifying details, change a plan, reserve a slot or say that rescheduling succeeded.

They can ask the scheduling desk whether another time or a more accessible way to review the options is available. They need not explain medical details here.

Check whether the entry is only an offer before calling it rescheduling. Drafting a question does not send a message or change an appointment.
