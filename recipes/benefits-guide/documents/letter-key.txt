Alderfen | Letter key: invitation, receipt or decision?
Fictional training material. Not an official service record or policy.

The heading determines the next route. Do not treat every letter as a decision.

An evidence invitation lists what the family is being asked to return and where. A delivery receipt is different: keep it and use its enquiry route to ask whether the desk needs anything else. Refer to the receipt privately at that desk. A receipt by itself gives no decision, payment date or promise. Do not send a second bundle just to ask about progress.

A decision notice gives reasons. To challenge a decision, read the review or appeal panel on that notice: check the stated time limit, contact route, and what to include. Identify the reason disputed and the supporting information relevant to that point. If the panel is missing or unreadable, ask the issuing desk for a complete copy and clarification promptly. The exercise supplies neither a universal deadline nor a personal appeal service.

Keep the receipt and use the enquiry route printed on it to ask about progress. A receipt is not a decision.

There is no need to paste a reference or a copy of the receipt into chat. Use the private channel specified by the issuing desk.

The written decision has a review or appeal panel. Check its route and time limit, then identify the reason the family disputes.

If that panel is unreadable, ask the issuing desk promptly for an accessible copy and clarification. This guide cannot lodge an appeal or predict the outcome.
